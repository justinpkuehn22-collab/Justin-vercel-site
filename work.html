Intuico Digital

A boutique consultancy. Custom software, websites, marketing: if a business needs it, we build it.

The problem

Small businesses get locked into long agency contracts, pay for retainers that don't move revenue, and can't get anyone on the phone when the site breaks.

Why I built it

We started it in 2021, while I was still at ASU. I wanted a shop that treats clients the way I'd want to be treated: straight answers and no busywork. We built the business from the ground up, and now we're scaling it to the level we always knew it could reach.

What's out there

Big agencies with 12-month contracts and account managers between you and the work, or freelancers who disappear after launch.

What makes it different

  • One partner for everything: custom software, sites, apps and marketing, so nothing gets lost between vendors.
  • No long-term lock-in contracts. Clients stay because it works.
  • Available 24/7, not "we'll circle back Monday."
  • We only recommend what delivers value, even when that means selling less.

Trydrobe

An AI stylist that picks your outfits, shops for you and plans your look for any event. Now an API for retailers too.

The problem

Getting dressed, shopping and planning an outfit for an event all take taste and time. Most people are guessing, and online stores can't help them decide.

Why we built it

My co-founder and I have zero fashion sense. We wanted AI to pick our outfits, help us shop and plan for events. Then we realized retailers needed the same tech, so we turned it into an API they can plug in.

What's out there

Style quizzes, subscription boxes and human stylists that don't scale. Retailers mostly get static product pages and basic recommendation widgets.

What makes it different

  • One AI stylist for outfits, shopping and event planning, not just "you might also like."
  • Started as the app we needed ourselves, and over 10,000 people now use it.
  • An API so bigger retailers can run virtual shopping for millions of customers on our tech.
  • Works with Shopify, where fashion brands already sell.

Guerilla Warfare

A cartoony battle royale where every player is an ape, and the whole city is your jungle gym.

The problem

Battle royales are stuck on the ground. In Fortnite, height comes from walls you build. In Apex, climbing is a short wall-scramble. Nobody owns vertical movement.

Why I'm building it

Ape movement alone turned Gorilla Tag into a $100M+ hit, but it's VR-only. The PC and console version of that feeling is wide open, and I want to build it.

What's out there

Fortnite, Apex and Call of Duty. Players get loudest about new movement: Black Ops 6's omnimovement reveal pulled 15M+ views, and Apex fans revolted when a movement trick was threatened.

How it plays

Guns win fights, apes finish them. Shooting is the most efficient way to win, and each ape class gets its own close-range move, like a hand smash or a run-up stab. Solos first, then squads of up to four.

What makes it different

  • Climb anything. Whole buildings, not a quick scramble. Movement replaces building as the skill that wins fights.
  • Run like an ape: a running style no human shooter has.
  • Rush from anywhere: drop off roofs and come up walls from angles nobody expects.
  • Fast, not frantic: about 1.1 to 1.25 times Fortnite's pace, with shorter matches on a big map.
  • Free to play with cosmetic skins, crowns and a season pass. Never pay-to-win.

Lead Machine

An outbound command center that doesn't just store leads, it coaches the call. Now my coworkers use it too.

The problem

A CRM tells you who to call. It doesn't tell you what to say, how warm the lead is, or what to send after the demo.

Why I built it

I'm a full-cycle AE. I wanted one screen that goes from first dial to booked demo to follow-up email without ten tabs open, and I built it so any rep could pick it up, not just me. Now reps at my job use it too.

What's out there

Enterprise CRMs built for managers' reports, and call-recording tools priced for whole sales orgs.

What makes it different

  • Built by a rep, for every rep, around the call itself. Coworkers adopted it on their own.
  • Guided call walkthroughs with talk tracks and battle cards in the moment.
  • Demo-call playbook and follow-up emails that pull the right case study.
  • Quota pacing so I know every day whether I'm ahead.

Poetry Shelf

A digital bookshelf for my own poetry.

The problem

My poems lived on a posting platform I didn't control. If it went away, so did the work.

Why I built it

I wanted a home for the poems I've written over the years, and a digital bookshelf felt right. Smart mirrors are becoming common, so one day this could live in a mirror I talk to and write poems with, not just an app.

What's out there

Social writing feeds built for likes, or plain notes apps with no soul.

What makes it different

  • A textured wooden bookshelf. Every poem is a book on it.
  • Each poem opens with a typewriter animation and signs itself in cursive.
  • I own the archive and can edit it any time.
  • Built with a smart-mirror future in mind: talk to it, write with it.
